Goal

Win the game by completing a line of 4 pieces or a 2x2 square of 4 pieces that share at least one common characteristic.

 Rules

This game is a variant of the official Quarto board game. The main differences in this puzzle are:

- The board is a 6x6 grid with removed corners (32 cells) instead of 4x4 (16 cells).
- There are 32 unique pieces instead of 16.
- Pieces have 5 characteristics instead of 4 (Light/Dark, Tall/Short, Circle/Square, Solid/Hollow, plus Glowing/Dim).
- You can also win by forming a 2x2 square (Advanced Quarto rule) or by having the most clustering points if the board fills up.

Each piece has its 5 characteristics encoded as a 5-bit integer (0 to 31):
- Bit 4 (Value 16): Core (0=Dim, 1=Glowing)
- Bit 3 (Value 8): Size (0=Short, 1=Tall)
- Bit 2 (Value 4): Color (0=Light, 1=Dark)
- Bit 1 (Value 2): Shape (0=Circle, 1=Square)
- Bit 0 (Value 1): Hole (0=Solid, 1=Hollow)

Examples of pieces:
• Piece 2 (Binary 00010) is Dim (0), Short (0), Light (0), Square (1), Solid (0).
• Piece 29 (Binary 11101) is Glowing (1), Tall (1), Dark (1), Circle (0), Hollow (1).

The game is alternating turn-based. To ensure varied games, the very first piece is chosen randomly by the game and given to Player 0. On their turn, the active player must place the piece they were given on an empty cell of the board, and then choose a new piece to give to their opponent. On the very last turn, the player simply places the last remaining piece.

Clustering Tie-Breaker: If the board fills up without any winner, a tie-breaker determines the winner. Players earn 1 tie-breaker point for every shared characteristic between the piece they just placed and its directly adjacent pieces (orthogonal and diagonal). The player with the highest tie-breaker score wins.


Victory Conditions
  • Place a piece that forms a horizontal, vertical, or diagonal line of 4 pieces sharing at least one characteristic.
  • Place a piece that forms a 2x2 square of 4 pieces sharing at least one characteristic (Advanced Quarto rule).
  • Have the highest Clustering Tie-Breaker score if the board is completely filled.
Defeat Conditions
  • Your program times out, crashes or outputs an invalid action.

 Game Input/Output

Initialization Input

Line 1: myId, your player ID (0 or 1).

Input for one game turn

Line 1: pieceToPlace, the ID (0 to 31) of the piece you must place.
Lines 2 to 7: 6 integers per line representing the board state. Empty cells are -1, the 4 non-playable corners are -2, and occupied cells contain the piece ID (0 to 31).
Line 8: N, the number of available pieces remaining.
Line 9: N space-separated integers representing the IDs of the available pieces.

Output for one game turn

A single line containing 2 or 3 space-separated integers: x y id

x, y : Coordinates (0 to 5) where to place the piece you were given.
id : The ID of the piece you want to give to your opponent (Turn 32 does not require an ID, output just x y).

Example : 2 1 0 (Places the current piece at 2,1 and gives piece 0 to the opponent)

Constraints

Response time for the first turn ≤ 1000 ms
Response time for subsequent turns ≤ 50 ms
